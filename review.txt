Reviewer A
------------
The authors propose ASA-SVRG, which employs an average of stochastic gradients and an average of the parameters. ASA-SVRG is compared with SGD, SVRG and NFG-SVRG.
The theoretical analysis appears to be reasonable. However, several points remain unclear, and the authors should address the following comments.

(1) The authors refer readers to the experimental results in the Appendix. However, the Appendix should contain supplementary information. The authors should explain the experimental results that support their claims in the main text.

(2) In the second paragraph of Section 4.1, the authors state:

"Consequently, the rapid decrease of the objective observed in these experiments is not itself the linear convergence
(the contraction under ?? < 1) guaranteed by Theorem 1."

However, the experimental results supporting this discussion are not clearly presented.
The authors should provide experimental results for different values of $\rho$ and discuss the relationship between $\rho$ and the actual convergence behavior.

(3) The theoretical analysis of the proposed method strongly relies on the assumptions of L-smooth and mu-strong convexity. However, it is unclear whether these assumptions hold for general machine learning problems, particularly for nonconvex optimization problems. When these assumptions are not satisfied, the proposed method is not necessarily guaranteed to reduce the deviation from the full gradient.
In fact, the experimental results in Sections 4.2 and 4.3 show that the proposed method performs worse than SGD in terms of loss and accuracy. Although the proposed method achieves better results than SVRG and NFG-SVRG and appears to reduce the approximation error, its advantages over SGD are not clearly demonstrated.

Therefore, the authors should provide more detailed experimental analyses and discussions in addition to comparing accuracy, loss, and approximation error. In particular, the authors should investigate the differences among the snapshot selection strategies, how the accumulated approximation error in the proposed method and NFG-SVRG affects training performance, and the computational cost associated with full-gradient calculations.

For example, in the second paragraph of Section 4.2, the authors state:

"In NFG-SVRG, the accumulated approximation error appears to affect the whole training as a bias of the corrected gradient, manifesting itself as a drop in accuracy and instability across trials."

However, this conclusion cannot be drawn from Fig. 2 alone. The authors should provide additional experimental results and discuss them to support this conclusion.

Reviewer B
------------
The paper proposes ASA-SVRG, which uses the average of the inner-loop iterates as the snapshot in NFG-SVRG. The authors show that this choice minimizes the snapshot-dependent term in an upper bound on the full-gradient approximation error. The experimental results demonstrate that ASA-SVRG achieves smaller approximation errors than NFG-SVRG while maintaining stable training on nonconvex image classification tasks.

The method is simple, the paper is clearly written, and its limitations are stated honestly. The following points can be addressed through textual revisions, and no additional experiments are required.

Major comments
1)
Averaged snapshots have already been used in SVRG-type methods. For example, Prox-SVRG [R1] uses the average of the inner-loop iterates as the snapshot and also starts the next stage from it, which is the same snapshot rule as in ASA-SVRG. Please cite the relevant prior work and clarify that the novelty of the present study lies in combining an averaged snapshot with NFG-SVRG and in analyzing the resulting approximation error.
[R1] Lin Xiao and Tong Zhang, "A Proximal Stochastic Gradient Method with Progressive Variance Reduction", SIAM Journal on Optimization, vol. 24, no. 4, pp. 2057-2075, 2014.
https://doi.org/10.1137/140961791

2)
The analysis assumes uniform sampling, whereas the experiments use random reshuffling. Please explicitly acknowledge this difference between the theoretical and experimental settings. In addition, no error floor is visible in Figs. 1(A) and B-2(A), where the objective error decreases to approximately 10^-15. Please explain this behavior and clarify that the error floor discussed in Section 3 refers to an upper bound. One possible explanation is that, under random reshuffling with K = N, the epoch-averaged gradient approaches the exact full gradient as the iterates converge, so the approximation error vanishes.


Minor comments
3) Section 3.1: "(Theorem 2)" should be corrected to "(Theorem 3)."
4) Figure 1(B): Please explain why the curves for NFG-SVRG and ASA-SVRG coincide exactly despite the use of different snapshots.
5) Please mention the instability of ASA-SVRG with small mini-batch sizes, as observed in Appendices B.2 and B.3, in the Abstract or Conclusions.
6) Please specify at which parameters (e.g., the snapshot z_s or the last inner-loop iterate) the loss and accuracy are evaluated. In addition, please indicate in the captions of Figs. B-4 and B-6 that the ASA-SVRG runs were terminated when divergence was observed.
7) Please use consistent notation and terminology throughout the paper, for example, "=" vs. "←" in the algorithms and "Test Accuracy" vs. "validation data."
